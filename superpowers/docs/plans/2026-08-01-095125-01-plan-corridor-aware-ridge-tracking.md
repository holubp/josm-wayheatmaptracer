# Corridor-Aware Ridge Tracking And Parallel-Trail Assignment

**Implementation status:** Implemented for the `v0.15.0` experimental release; `LEGACY_V02` remains the default pending the promotion gate below.

**Goal:** Add an experimental corridor-aware tracker that follows the stable center of broad, medium, and sparse heatmap trails, preserves evidence-backed turns, approaches endpoints without artificial kinks, and distinguishes lane-level strands from separate parallel carriageways.
**Why planning is required:** The change spans sampling, probabilistic evidence, longitudinal association, geometry optimization, OSM topology context, diagnostics, UI configuration, and real-world regression calibration. It must coexist with the known-working v0.2-compatible tracker until the replacement has objective and subjective evidence.
**Acceptance:** `LEGACY_V02` remains the default and produces unchanged regression results; `CORRIDOR_AWARE` is selectable as an experimental tracker and works for both managed-tile and visible-layer sampling; signal handling changes continuously from strong through medium to sparse without hard mode switches; broad stationary corridors stay centered to within 0.5 source pixel RMS despite pixel noise; sustained sine/switchback amplitude is retained at 90% or better; persistent low-intensity strands below the current absolute peak threshold remain traceable while isolated noise does not become applicable; multi-lane strands can produce one parent corridor, divided carriageways remain separate candidates, and ambiguous cases remain available for preview; fixed endpoints are exact and approached without unsupported kinks, movable junctions stay near their source position, candidates remain subject to existing structural safety checks, and no neighboring OSM object is edited implicitly; all new decisions are present in redacted debug exports; full tests and the offline fixture regression pass before publishing `v0.15.0` with `wayheatmaptracer.jar`.

### Outcome 2: Retain complete normalized intensity profiles without breaking legacy peaks

- Work: Extend `RenderedHeatmapSampler.CrossSectionProfile` with immutable per-offset samples containing offset, native intensity, B3 intensity, B5 intensity, and whether the sample is inside the raster. Preserve existing constructors used by legacy code/tests by supplying an empty sample list. Populate full samples in both `sampleProfilesOnScaledRaster` and `sampleProfilesOnAggregatedScaledRasters`; aggregation must continue to happen at scalar-intensity level before profile construction. Add profile statistics that separately describe signal-existence confidence and localization confidence. Signal existence must use absolute prominence above a robust local noise floor, gradient evidence, native/B3/B5 agreement, and later longitudinal support. Localization confidence must use corridor width, edge balance, nested-center agreement, and source-pixel uncertainty. Normalize structural profile levels against `noiseFloor + q * (maximum - noiseFloor)` so medium and sparse evidence passes through the same calculations as strong evidence. Do not discard a profile solely because it falls into a named strong/medium/weak class; transparent, off-raster, and numerically empty samples remain distinct unsupported states.
- Risks/open questions: Relative normalization can amplify empty-image noise. Provisional low-amplitude observations must remain non-applicable until longitudinal persistence raises signal-existence confidence; retain a tiny numerical-empty floor only to avoid division instability, not as a user-visible intensity class boundary.

### Outcome 3: Extract robust nested corridor observations from every profile

- Work: Add `CorridorBand` and `CorridorProfile` records plus a `CorridorExtractor` in `service/`. For each profile, identify connected intervals at initial relative levels `q={0.60, 0.72, 0.84, 0.92}` above the local noise floor. Associate nested intervals from low shoulder to high core. Calculate each band's center as the weighted median of interval boundary midpoints, biased toward high-core levels; do not use an exact single-pixel maximum or raw intensity centroid as the primary center. Export shoulder bounds, core bounds, nested centers, width, peak, valley depths, signal-existence confidence, localization confidence, and uncertainty. Preserve multiple disjoint bands. If two high-core components share a low-level parent, emit child bands and a parent-band hypothesis rather than deciding immediately whether they are lanes or carriageways. Keep the existing `CrossSectionPeak` extraction unchanged for `LEGACY_V02`.
- Risks/open questions: The initial relative levels are calibration constants, not immutable truth. Keep them package-private and change them only with raw-profile and fixture regression coverage.

### Outcome 4: Associate elementary heat strands longitudinally without switching identities

- Work: Add `CorridorTrack` and `CorridorTracker`. Build a longitudinal graph whose nodes are child `CorridorBand` observations and whose edges connect adjacent profiles plus gaps of up to two unsupported profiles. Edge cost must compare georeferenced sampled-raster center positions, projected interval overlap, source-pixel-normalized displacement, width change, left/right ordering, signal existence, and localization uncertainty. Compare actual center points (`anchor + normal * offset`), not raw offsets alone, because profile normals rotate on curves. Retain the best distinct longitudinal tracks with deterministic IDs and stable ordering. Bridge internal no-signal gaps using evidence on both sides; at terminal gaps, use a damped continuation of the last supported slope rather than snapping to zero or inventing a new mode. Keep separate persistent tracks when two parallel modes remain distinct, and reject one-profile side excursions unless their evidence exceeds the continuity cost over multiple profiles.
- Risks/open questions: Bound graph size by retaining only the best observations per connected profile component and a documented maximum number of partial states. Exceeding the bound must yield a diagnostic ambiguity warning, never silently drop the strongest established track.
- Verify: `sh gradlew test --tests org.openstreetmap.josm.plugins.wayheatmaptracer.service.CorridorTrackerTest`

### Outcome 5: Form parent corridors for lanes while retaining separate carriageways

- Work: Add a longitudinal grouping pass over elementary `CorridorTrack` objects. Calculate separation relative to child widths and source-pixel size, the normalized valley ratio between children, common-parent-envelope persistence, child co-occurrence, and left/right-order stability. Use initial interpretation bands: valley ratio at or above `0.65` supports a combined parent, at or below `0.40` supports separate tracks, and the middle range remains ambiguous. A grouping decision must persist over at least five profiles and at least 60% of their common supported distance. For a parent corridor, center from the midpoint structure of the combined outer/core envelopes rather than activity-weighted intensity, so a busier lane does not pull the road center sideways. In ambiguous cases emit the combined parent and child tracks as separately labeled preview candidates. Never infer lane/carriageway semantics from distance alone.
- Risks/open questions: Heatmap-only evidence cannot prove lane versus carriageway identity in every case. Ambiguity is an expected result and must not be hidden by ranking only one candidate.
- Verify: `sh gradlew test --tests org.openstreetmap.josm.plugins.wayheatmaptracer.service.CorridorGroupingTest`

### Outcome 6: Optimize a stable centerline inside the selected corridor

- Work: Add `CorridorCenterlineOptimizer`, separate from the legacy `RidgeTracker.smoothOffsets`. For each corridor track, construct allowed offset states from sampled offsets inside the shoulder envelope plus inserted robust core centers and interval boundaries. Solve globally with dynamic programming whose state retains the previous two offsets, so costs can include data fit, first-difference continuity, and second-difference lateral acceleration. Normalize all motion terms by physical profile spacing and source-pixel size. Data cost must reward native and B3/B5 intensity agreement, prefer the robust core center according to localization confidence, stay finite throughout a flat core, and rise sharply outside the core/shoulder envelope. Increase stabilization continuously as localization uncertainty rises; decrease acceleration cost only when at least three consecutive profiles show consistent center motion with cross-scale support. Keep a nonzero acceleration cost even at supported turns. Re-evaluate every optimized point against its original full intensity profile and mark a candidate unsafe if unexplained optimization places it outside its selected corridor. Do not run a free moving average after this optimization.
- Risks/open questions: A global smoothness term can narrow real sine-shaped geometry. Acceptance tests must enforce retained bend amplitude, and optimizer scoring must let sustained data evidence dominate regularization rather than increasing a global smoothing strength until tests pass.
- Verify: `sh gradlew test --tests org.openstreetmap.josm.plugins.wayheatmaptracer.service.CorridorCenterlineOptimizerTest --tests org.openstreetmap.josm.plugins.wayheatmaptracer.service.CorridorTrackerTest`

### Outcome 7: Make strong, medium, and sparse evidence a continuous confidence spectrum

- Work: Calibrate the shared confidence functions using synthetic profiles spanning the full intensity range. A narrow persistent strand may have modest signal-existence confidence but high localization confidence; a broad saturated plateau has high existence confidence but larger localization uncertainty; medium cases interpolate continuously. Remove new-path dependencies on the legacy absolute gates `0.14`, `0.20`, and `0.30`. Require weak observations to accumulate support over a physical longitudinal window before they can create an applicable candidate. Use lighter B3/native evidence when a strand is narrower than approximately one source pixel, while broad observations can rely more on B5 and nested boundaries. Preserve low-intensity paths through short gaps, suppress isolated wandering strands beside a stronger center, and expose the two confidence values separately in candidate evidence.
- Risks/open questions: Do not tune only to synthetic RGB values. Final constants must be checked against exported palette calibration tiles for every source color and the all-color aggregate mapping.
- Verify: `sh gradlew test --tests org.openstreetmap.josm.plugins.wayheatmaptracer.service.CorridorConfidenceTest --tests org.openstreetmap.josm.plugins.wayheatmaptracer.service.HeatmapFixtureArchiveTest`

### Outcome 8: Incorporate endpoint and junction boundary conditions before geometry is built

- Work: Add `EndpointConstraint`/`JunctionContext` construction in `AlignmentService` and consume it in `CorridorCenterlineOptimizer`. A fixed endpoint is an exact position constraint (zero lateral displacement in its endpoint profile) plus a soft approach-tangent cost over a physical 10–20 metre window. A movable endpoint or shared junction uses heatmap evidence plus a strong prior to the original coordinate and a hard maximum displacement no greater than `min(searchHalfWidthMeters, 10 m)`; log both prior and applied displacement. Derive approach direction from the selected way and available connected-way geometry, but treat it as a soft constraint when heatmap turn evidence is persistent. If a fixed endpoint is outside the selected heat corridor, generate the least-curved constrained transition, mark profiles that necessarily leave the corridor, and show a preview warning. Keep `guardFixedAnchorTurns`, endpoint-cluster pruning, and self-intersection cleanup as final safety checks, not as the primary way to construct the connection.
- Risks/open questions: Moving a shared node changes every referring way. Keep junction movement opt-in, preserve the existing selected/shared node, and do not simplify or replace it.
- Verify: `sh gradlew test --tests org.openstreetmap.josm.plugins.wayheatmaptracer.service.AlignmentServiceTest --tests org.openstreetmap.josm.plugins.wayheatmaptracer.service.GeometryPostProcessorTest --tests org.openstreetmap.josm.plugins.wayheatmaptracer.util.MoveNodesCommandTest --tests org.openstreetmap.josm.plugins.wayheatmaptracer.util.ReplaceWaySegmentCommandTest`
